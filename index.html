Why Sleeper's Median Forecast Fails

Sleeper computes its projected median by taking the static point projections of the 10 teams, sorting them, and averaging #5 and #6.

Order-Statistic Selection Bias: When the top 4 teams lock in high scores and 5–6 bubble teams are chasing the #5 spot with volatile players left to play, the eventual #5 score is the maximum of those chasing teams (E[max(X₅..X₁₀)] > max(E[X₅]..E[X₁₀])).

√t Volatility Scaling & Right-Skewed Scoring

Sleeper's Flaw: Scales standard deviation nearly linearly with remaining clock fraction (SD ∝ t_rem) and uses symmetric Gaussian assumptions.

Our Fix: Because football scoring is a stochastic jump process over drives, remaining variance scales with t_eff, meaning standard deviation scales with √t_eff (at 25% time left, 50% of full-game SD remains!). Uses an exact mean-0, variance-1 right-skewed normal transformation calibrated by position.

Correlated Stacks & Shared Game Environments

Sleeper's Flaw: Treats every starter on every roster as statistically independent.

Our Fix: In each of our 10,000 Monte Carlo trials, we draw latent NFL Game Pace factors (Z_game), Team Passing factors (Z_pass), and Team Rushing factors (Z_rush). Same-team QB–WR/TE stacks share ρ ≈ +0.38 correlation.
